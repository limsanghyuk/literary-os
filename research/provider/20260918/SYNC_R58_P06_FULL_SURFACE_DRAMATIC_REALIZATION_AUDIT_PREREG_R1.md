- institutional / group pressure;

- mistake / unintended consequence;

- status reversal / authority transfer;
- aftermath / payoff / debt creation;
- quiet relational accumulation / subtext.

A scene may carry multiple mechanisms.

## Frozen critical defects

Any of the following is a critical defect candidate and must be supported by scene evidence:

1. `EXPLANATION_SUBSTITUTES_FOR_DRAMA`
   The direction tells the relationship/theme/state change while behavior/dialogue does not make it legible.

2. `ROLE_SLOT_CHARACTERIZATION`
   Characters are interchangeable function-holders with insufficiently distinct desire/judgment/concealment/voice/pressure response.

3. `SEMANTIC_SCENE_TEMPLATE_REPEAT`
   Different wording/entities conceal substantially the same scene transaction pattern.

4. `PROCESSING_WITHOUT_DRAMATIC_RESOLUTION`

5. `SCENE_NECESSITY_UNPROVEN`
   Removing/merging a scene causes no material loss in event, relationship, information, rhythm, emotional accumulation, or later causality.

6. `OUTPUT_ONLY_RECONSTRUCTION_FAILURE`

7. `PLAN_SURFACE_STATE_MISMATCH`
   Surface outcome differs materially from the architecture/state ledger or invents false closure.

- decision rule;
- speech rhythm / lexical tendencies;

IDs such as C01 are not themselves a failure; anonymity and characterization are evaluated separately.

## Choice–Resistance–Cost test

A major dramatic transaction is considered fully realized only when the screenplay provides enough evidence for:
1. a character objective or pressure;
2. resistance or incompatibility;
3. a meaningful choice/action;
4. a cost/risk/consequence;
5. a resulting state change.

Not every atmospheric/bridging scene must contain all five, but due-now resolution cannot pass solely by status update.
