## Scientific interpretation
The intervention produced a strong but incomplete signal. It consistently helped procedural access, investigation, time-pressure transit, evidence-ownership negotiation, status/power conflict, internal-preservation tension, deadline escalation and field evidence-control scenes.

The losses reveal a specific boundary: simply increasing direction and compressing dialogue can damage **character voice, comic/lived rhythm and ensemble social texture**. S26, S31 and S36 retained stronger human texture in Control because their dialogue carried character-specific humor, social friction and everyday rhythm rather than mere exposition. S27 showed that direct emotional language can sometimes function as relationship action rather than audience explanation.

Therefore the correct doctrine is NOT `less dialogue = better` or `more direction = better`.
The emerging doctrine is:
- remove audience-facing explanatory dialogue;
- preserve dialogue that performs character action, voice, humor, intimacy, conflict or social texture;
- externalize subtext into playable direction where that increases dramatic information;
- do not over-compress dialogue merely because direction can carry the beat.

This distinction must be built into the next intervention/reliability Selector.
