# 3. WHY E6-R2 WAS NARROWED

The developer manually reviewed all 50 E6-R2 scenes and identified construct-validity defects that original formal gates missed.

Major findings:

1. **Scene summary inside dialogue.**
   Characters sometimes verbalized scene-plan/state information or their own psychology instead of speaking as people.

2. **Reported agency instead of enacted agency.**
   Important refusals, approvals, relationship changes and decisions were sometimes explained by another character instead of being performed by the responsible character.

3. **Fixed repeated topology.**
   All 50 scenes used exactly four speaking characters and six utterances. The 300 utterances contained extreme repeated-line/template behavior.

4. **Crisis-process omission.**
   Problems often jumped directly to a solution declaration without `attempt -> failure/new obstacle -> harder choice/cost -> consequence`.

5. **Spatial / communication ambiguity.**
   Field and control-center characters sometimes appeared to manipulate the same information/props without clear co-location or remote mode.

6. **Civilian / stakeholder agency weakness.**
   Large affected populations appeared mainly as state counts rather than people with direct choices, resistance, fear and consequence.

7. **Korean surface defects.**
   The developer found 139 name-particle/postposition errors in the reviewed screenplay.

Conclusion:
E6-R2 remains `PASS_UNDER_ORIGINAL_FROZEN_GATES`, but those gates were insufficient for `BROADCAST_DRAMATURGY_CONSTRUCT_VALIDITY`.

## Craft rules
- dialogue must not explain story/state to the audience;

- direct emotion labels should be avoided when performance can show the beat;
- decisive actions must be performed by the responsible character, not reported by another character;
- no fixed cast size or utterance count;

- field/remote spatial mode must be clear;
