## 0. Purpose

This plan responds to a newly confirmed system-level regression: the project had already researched and designed a rich `Series -> Episode -> Sequence -> Scene` hierarchy and a writer-room/showrunner operating loop, but the active E3/E6 experimental planners did not consistently consume that full architecture.

The goal is not to invent a new screenwriting formula and not to imitate a specific human drama's story content. The goal is to restore and causally verify the project's original Showrunner Operating Layer:

`Series Identity/Long Arc -> Current Narrative State -> Episode Function -> Character/Relationship/Thread Debts -> Multi-story Allocation -> Ensemble/Event Ownership -> Candidate Architecture Portfolio -> Episode Architecture -> Sequence Value Shifts -> Scene Contracts -> Surface -> Diagnostics -> Responsible-Ancestor Replan -> State Commit/Carry`.

Human-written drama analysis is used only as structural/craft evidence. Names, incidents, plot solutions, dialogue, scene order, and distinctive story content must not be copied into a new work.

## 1. Why E3 surface was better than E6 surface

### 1.1 E3 positive partial evidence
E3-R2 was not human-level architecture, but its fresh series seed and scene contracts contained more heterogeneous dramatic affordances:
- civilians with concrete immediate needs rather than only population counts;
- resident representative, child/parent vulnerability, vendor, reporter, administrator, field safety officer, budget/contract officer;
- direct family/conflict-of-interest pressure around Sumin;
- direct institutional pressure around Seoha and Park Jungwoo;
- relationship movement enacted through encounters rather than only recorded as state labels;
- varied scene participation and dialogue topology in the prior full-script audit.

E3 still had a major limitation: most secondary lines ultimately branched from one parent operational objective, `shelter 28 households tonight`. Therefore E3 is a **positive partial control**, not the target architecture.

### 1.2 E6 regression
E6-R2 increased formal/mechanical controllability while reducing dramatic freedom:
- episode planning contracted toward `objective + initial/target state + sequence goal/obstacle/turn/state_delta`;
- human-corpus fields such as explicit episode function, act/movement structure, major turning point, sequence POV/owner, value shift taxonomy, story-debt allocation and runtime allocation were not all load-bearing contracts;
- formal qualification emphasized exact state transition, leakage=0, fixed scale and deterministic validation;
- after E6-R1 internal-token leakage, boundary/validator repair correctly removed internal state tokens but did not restore dramaturgical diversity;
- the E6-R2 renderer used a fixed 4-speaking-character / 6-utterance scene skeleton, creating repeated scene shape and semantic-plan paraphrase;
- whole-episode dramaturgy diversity, direct agency, crisis-process depth, civilian agency and topology diversity were not hard gates in the original E6-R2 qualification.

Therefore E6 passed the gates that were measured while regressing on dimensions that were not adequately measured.

### 1.3 Causal diagnosis
The regression is modeled as two coupled failures:

1. `ARCHITECTURE_CONSUMPTION_REGRESSION`
   - rich DB/showrunner knowledge existed;
   - active planner contracts collapsed toward operational task decomposition;
   - one operational objective could parent most downstream sequences/scenes.

2. `SURFACE_REALIZATION_REGRESSION`
   - deterministic/formal hardening removed lexical/internal-state leakage;
   - fixed renderer topology and semantic-plan paraphrase replaced dramatic interaction;
   - downstream validators did not detect this construct-validity loss.

The next experiment must prove both layers, separately and end-to-end.

## 2. Historical evidence to restore, not reinvent

Mandatory historical contracts:
- `FullSeriesArc -> EpisodeArc -> SequenceBlueprint -> SceneBlueprint` four-layer hierarchy;
- sequence = `goal -> obstacle -> value_shift/turn`, not location grouping or workflow step;
- episode function in long series trajectory;
- act/major-movement structure, midpoint/reversal/climax and irreversible choice;
- character/relationship/thread/subplot debts from prior state;
- ensemble/social-ecology and event ownership;
- candidate portfolio before sequence commitment;
- responsible-ancestor bidirectional replanning;
- accepted-state commit/carry only after full validation.

These were already present in prior project research and are now mandatory main-path consumers.

## 3. Human-corpus use doctrine

Human corpus analysis is a `CRAFT STRUCTURE REFERENCE`, not a story template.

Allowed transfer:
- distributions of sequence ownership and POV diversity;
- act/turn/value-shift patterns;
- ways independent character/relationship/institutional threads collide;
- episode-function and long-arc concepts;
- scene-function and sequence-budget principles;
- negative patterns and mismatch evidence.

Prohibited transfer:
- specific names, incidents, solutions, dialogue, unique scene order, distinctive plot twists or character backstories;
- nearest-neighbor episode skeleton copying;
- one-to-one donor substitution.

Every new architecture must have a provenance ledger showing that retrieved human evidence affected only abstract craft decisions.

## 4. Research questions

RQ1. Does restoring the full Showrunner Architecture Packet causally prevent `one operational objective -> procedural checklist` collapse?

RQ2. Can the planner create partially independent character/relationship/institutional story lines that would retain meaningful pressure even if the main incident changed, while still converging causally within the episode?

RQ3. Can multiple architecture candidates differ in meaningful story architecture rather than surface wording?

RQ4. Does the selected top-down architecture survive sequence lowering, scene lowering and final surface realization without collapsing back into a single task chain?

RQ5. Can the same reverse-analysis stack used on human scripts recover the intended episode function, owners, value shifts, story debts and turning points from the generated final script?

RQ6. Does the new surface retain E3's stronger direct-human drama qualities while eliminating E3's single-parent-objective limitation and E6's fixed-template regression?

RQ7. Do independent blind evaluators recognize the final work as a coherent, varied broadcast episode rather than an operations log or plan paraphrase?

## 5. Hypotheses

H1. Full Showrunner consumption will outperform an objective/state-only planner on story autonomy, ownership diversity, cross-axis causal influence, turn diversity and anti-task-collapse.

H2. A candidate portfolio with different event owners, relationship movement, information timing and midpoint/climax choices will produce architecture-level diversity before any scene prose exists.

H3. An accepted architecture will preserve its independent story axes and value shifts through 45+ scene lowering with no orphan scene and no protagonist-only collapse.

H4. R3-C Show-Only Direction plus anti-template surface rules will prevent the E6 fixed-4-person/6-utterance regression while preserving plan fidelity.

H5. Forward-Reverse Architecture Closure will recover the planned multi-story architecture from the final surface at high fidelity.

H6. Independent blind whole-episode/scene evaluation will pass only if both architecture and surface gates pass; internal mechanical PASS alone is insufficient.

### R3-E5 — Scene Contract Lowering
Lower to broadcast scale only after E4 PASS.

Scale is architecture-driven, not fixed-formula driven:
- minimum >=9 sequences and >=45 scenes;
- expected broadcast range 9-12 sequences / 45-60 scenes;
- >=35,000 Korean characters at surface stage;
- larger counts allowed if justified by architecture.

Every scene contract requires:
- owner/focality;
- dramatic function;
- participating characters;
- conflict/pressure;
- action/decision owner;
- information action;
- relationship action where relevant;
- physical/subtext target;
- entry/exit state;
- parent sequence obligation;
- downstream consequence.

No fixed cast size, utterance count or dialogue/action ratio.

### R3-E6 — Whole-Episode Surface Realization
Render only from sealed scene contracts.

Mandatory inherited rules:

- direction shows playable behavior and does not explain dialogue meaning;
- decisive actions are enacted by the responsible character;
- no internal field/enum/meta leakage;
- no fixed scene topology;
- crisis processes cannot skip attempt/failure/cost when the architecture requires them;
- spatial/remote communication mode must be explicit;
- affected civilians/stakeholders act directly where dramatically relevant;
- Korean particles/honorifics/naming checked.

### R3-E7 — Forward-Reverse Architecture Closure
This is a new mandatory gate.

After surface completion, hide the original plan from the analysis path and run the same or equivalent reverse-analysis stack used on human scripts.

Recover from the generated screenplay:
- EpisodeArc;
- act/movement structure;
- dramatic question;
- central conflict axis;
- episode function;
- major turning point;
- SequenceBlueprints;
- POV/owners;
- goals/obstacles;

- turn types/classes;

Critical fail examples:
- planned four/three/etc. story lines collapse to one operational chain in the rendered script;
- supporting-story owner disappears from reverse analysis;
- midpoint/climax is not observable in the final surface;
- planned relationship movement exists only as state metadata, not screenplay evidence;
- sequence value shifts cannot be recovered from scenes.

This gate operationalizes the original reason for reverse-analyzing human scripts: a valid plan must remain structurally legible in the finished work.

## 7. Key metrics and anti-Goodhart policy

Metrics are diagnostic, not a fixed writing formula.

Do NOT hard-code:
- exactly four story lines;
- exactly four POV owners;
- exactly ten sequences;
- exactly fifty scenes;
- exactly one relationship beat per sequence;
- fixed midpoint percentage;
- fixed dialogue counts.

Use human-corpus distributions as calibration bands and anomaly detectors, not as templates.

Strong anomaly examples:
- all scenes have identical cast size/utterance count;
- one operational objective directly explains nearly every sequence;
- all supporting stories vanish if the main task is removed;
- all sequence exits are verification/approval rather than value changes;
- relationship movement is stated in metadata but not recoverable from screenplay action.
