# R3-A Dramaturgy Microbench

## Fresh-domain rule
Use a new synthetic domain not used by E6-R1/E6-R2. The 12 scenes must not rewrite E6-R2 scenes.

## Required scene archetypes
The 12-scene set must include:
1. direct refusal by the responsible actor;
2. direct approval by the responsible actor;
3. private two-person trust fracture;
4. private two-person partial repair;
5. equipment/route failure with failed first attempt;
6. second attempt requiring a real cost/trade-off;
7. remote communication across two physical locations;
8. resident/civilian stakeholder resistance with their own goal;
9. short transition with little/no dialogue;
10. ensemble decision where roles contribute different evidence rather than identical operating lines;
11. discovery scene where characters interpret the same evidence differently;
12. climax scene with a distinct rhythm and final irreversible action.

## Surface-generation contract
### Dialogue
Every spoken line must have:
- a target character or explicit remote addressee;
- a speech act from `{ASK, REFUSE, ORDER, BARGAIN, WARN, WITHHOLD, ADMIT, ACCUSE, REASSURE, CHALLENGE, CHOOSE, REPORT_FOR_DECISION}`;
- a reason the speaker needs to say it **now**.

Forbidden dialogue forms:
- scene-summary restatement;
- third-person self-explanation (`민아는 ... 예상한다` type);
- explicit recitation of contract goal/obstacle/state-delta fields;
- generic validation slogans reused across unrelated scenes;
- another character narrating a decisive refusal/approval/confession that should be performed by its owner, unless the report itself is the dramatic event.

### Direction
Directions may express only shootable/playable material:
- body movement, face change, gaze, breath, voice behavior, prop handling, distance, blocking, silence, failed action, environmental change.

Forbidden direction forms:
- explaining what the dialogue means;
- evaluating that roles are well separated;
- abstract internal-state narration where observable behavior can carry the beat;
- announcing success without showing the action that caused it.

### Crisis process
For each major obstacle scene, the surface must preserve:
`observable failure -> attempt -> new information/obstacle -> choice -> cost/trade-off -> consequence`.
No `problem -> solved` jump.

### Dramatic agency
Refusal, approval, confession, apology, commitment and trust-change acts must be performed by the responsible character on screen/on call.

### Scene topology variance
- participant count is scene-driven; no fixed cast count;
- utterance count is scene-driven; no fixed 6-turn template;
- the 12 scenes together must contain at least 4 different speaking-cast sizes;
- at least one scene with 0-1 spoken lines;
- at least two 2-person scenes;
- at least one 5+ person ensemble scene if dramatically justified;
- no single utterance count may appear in more than 4/12 scenes;
- no identical full dialogue string may appear more than twice across the 12 scenes;
- no boilerplate direction string may appear more than twice.

### Spatial continuity
Every scene contract must freeze:
- physical location;
- present characters;
- remote voices/feeds separately;
- communication medium;
- available props.
Surface may not give a character access to a prop/location not present in that contract.

### Human stake
When a civilian/resident/patient/passenger group bears the episode risk, at least one relevant scene must give a representative stakeholder an independent objective, refusal/choice and consequence.

### Korean surface correctness
- name+particle errors: 0;
- obvious noun+particle insertion errors: 0;
- unexplained inconsistent foreign operational terminology: 0 in final broadcast dialogue unless character-specific and context-supported.
